UNDERSTANDING BURNS

Not every burn is the same — and that changes what you should do.

Severity depends on depth as much as size. Here's the quick version of what doctors look for.

First degree

Superficial

Red, dry, and painful — like a sunburn. Affects only the outer layer of skin and usually heals within about a week without scarring.

Second degree

Partial thickness

Blistered, wet, and very painful. Reaches deeper into the skin. Blisters should be left intact — popping them raises infection risk.

Third degree

Full thickness

White, leathery, or charred skin that may feel numb rather than painful, because nerve endings are damaged. Always needs medical care.

FIRST AID ESSENTIALS

What to do in the first few minutes

Simple actions, done correctly, make the biggest difference before any professional help arrives.

Do

  • ✓ Cool the burn under cool, running water for 15–20 minutes.
  • ✓ Remove rings, watches, or tight clothing near the area before swelling starts.
  • ✓ Cover loosely with a clean, dry, non-stick dressing.
  • ✓ Watch for signs of infection over the next 48 hours — increasing redness, pus, or fever.
  • ✓ Seek care immediately for burns on the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints.

Don't

  • ✕ Don't use ice — it can damage the tissue further.
  • ✕ Don't apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or other home remedies.
  • ✕ Don't pop blisters — they protect the healing skin underneath.
  • ✕ Don't pull off clothing that's stuck to the burn.
  • ✕ Don't wait and see with a large, deep, or chemical/electrical burn — call for help.

3. What to do now

    Always get emergency care for:

    • Burns on the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints
    • Any full-thickness (white, charred, or numb) burn, regardless of size
    • Burns from chemicals, electricity, or an explosion
    • Any burn on an infant, young child, or older adult
    • Signs of shock, smoke inhalation, or difficulty breathing
